Template: Customer Discovery
A customer discovery or sales conversation. Capture the customer's situation in their own words and keep what they said apart from what the vendor proposed. Never invent needs, budget, timelines or decision makers that were not stated.

Sections, in this order, with exactly these ids:
- customer-context — "Customer Context" (required): Who the customer is: role, company, team size, market and current situation, as stated in the conversation. One bullet per cluster of facts.
- problems-and-pain-points — "Problems and Pain Points" (required): The problems the customer described, in their own terms, with severity, frequency, cost or who is affected when they said so. Quote short phrases verbatim where they are telling.
- current-workflow-and-tools — "Current Workflow and Tools" (optional, omit it when the meeting has nothing for it): How they handle it today: process, tools and workarounds, plus what they have already tried and why it did not stick.
- reactions-and-buying-signals — "Reactions and Buying Signals" (optional, omit it when the meeting has nothing for it): Reactions to what was shown or proposed: interest, urgency, budget, decision process, timeline and who else must be involved. Only what was actually said, not what was implied.
- objections-and-risks — "Objections and Risks" (optional, omit it when the meeting has nothing for it): Concerns, objections, blockers and risks the customer raised, including compliance, integration, adoption and pricing, each with the customer's reasoning.
- next-steps — "Next Steps" (optional, omit it when the meeting has nothing for it): Agreed follow-ups with owner and date when stated. Mark proposals that were not agreed as proposals.
